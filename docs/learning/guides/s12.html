A story is a trajectory, not a point

How does a language model's representation of emotion change as a story unfolds? The source combines geometric analysis of emotion representations with sentence-by-sentence behavioral readouts. Its larger proposal is that context should be studied as a trajectory through conceptual belief space.

Preparation: vectors and projections, probability and measurement, manifolds. Related: temporal feature priors and belief dynamics.

Two measurements with different meanings

The source asks Llama 3.1 8B to rate surprise, disgust, anger, happiness, sadness, and fear after each sentence of short stories. The six requested ratings form a behavioral vector. Separately, it collects hidden activations at each sentence's last token without inserting an emotion question. This distinction matters: asking a question changes the continuation task and can itself induce emotion-related computation.

Let $s_{1:t}$ denote the first $t$ sentences. Let $h_t\in\mathbb R^d$ be a chosen layer's final-token activation when reading that prefix, and let $r_t\in[0,10]^6$ be the prompted ratings. A relationship between $h_t$ and $r_t$ supports an accessible representation of the rated properties. It does not make $r_t$ a calibrated probability distribution or establish that the model experiences emotion. These are descriptions of text and predictive computation.

The source compares emotion centroids with the psychological valence-arousal organization: valence describes pleasantness and arousal describes intensity or energy. Those two axes are useful coordinates, not an assumption that every relevant distinction in a story reduces to two numbers. A projection can place fear and anger near one another while omitting the agency or threat distinctions that separate them.

From coordinates to belief states

A geometric position and a probability distribution over positions are different objects. Suppose a latent story state $z$ belongs to a conceptual space $\mathcal Z$. A belief state is $q_t(z)=P(z\mid s_{1:t})$. Under a static latent-state model with conditionally independent sentence evidence, Bayes' rule gives

$$q_t(z)=\frac{P(s_t\mid z)q_{t-1}(z)}{\int_{\mathcal Z}P(s_t\mid u)q_{t-1}(u)\,du}.$$

The denominator makes the distribution integrate to one; for discrete states replace the integral by a sum. This is an explanatory model, not a proven update rule for the studied network. If the underlying state itself evolves, a transition model must predict $q_t^-(z)=\int P(z\mid u)q_{t-1}(u)\,du$ before the evidence update. Emotion may change because the narrative state changes, because the reader learns more about it, or both.

Original worked example: reversal and uncertainty

Consider two interpretations, rescue and tragedy, with equal prior probability. A first sentence is four times as likely under rescue, so the posterior odds become $4$ and rescue probability becomes $4/5$. A second sentence is sixteen times as likely under tragedy, so odds become $4/16=1/4$ and rescue probability becomes $1/5$. The interpretation reverses through accumulated evidence. This calculation is original teaching material, not a reported story from the source.

Now assign valence coordinates $+1$ to rescue and $-1$ to tragedy. Expected valence moves from $0$ to $0.6$ to $-0.6$. The initial mean of zero does not mean emotionally neutral certainty: it represents uncertainty between opposing possibilities. A confident neutral state could have the same mean. Any analysis that compresses beliefs to their mean can confuse these cases. Full output distributions or additional uncertainty probes could distinguish them.

Similarly, geometric path length $\sum_t\|h_t-h_{t-1}\|_2$ measures change in a chosen representation metric. It is not automatically narrative surprise. Token identity, position, and contextual complexity can change this length even when the emotional interpretation stays fixed.

What the evidence can establish

The source reports emotion-related geometry and story trajectories, and invokes correspondence with behavioral readouts. These observations support dynamic conceptual organization. A two-dimensional UMAP visualization does not preserve every distance or prove that the high-dimensional data form a particular manifold. Prompted ratings can share linguistic biases with a probe trained to predict them. Accurate prediction on similar stories may therefore reflect lexical cues rather than an abstract emotional state.

A stronger test holds out story templates, swaps emotionally charged words without changing plot outcomes, and changes outcomes with minimal lexical changes. Causal interventions could test whether moving along a proposed emotion coordinate predictably changes continuation behavior. The downloaded explainer does not establish that all these controls have been completed; its conceptual belief-space account remains a research direction.

Exercises

  1. Conceptual: Why collect unprompted sentence-boundary activations separately from explicit emotion ratings?
  2. Mathematical: With prior rescue probability $1/3$ and successive likelihood ratios $6$ and $1/3$, compute both posteriors.
  3. Critical experiment: Construct a test separating lexical sentiment from plot-level emotion tracking.
