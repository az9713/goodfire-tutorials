When a feature needs several coordinates

The central question is whether a featurizer can recover a concept together with its internal variation, rather than assign a separate label to each local fragment. The source introduces Block-Sparse Featurizers (BSFs), applies them to vision representations, and argues that sparsity should select subspaces rather than individual directions.

Preparation: linear algebra, network activations, geometry and sparse coding. Read SAE capture regimes for the problem BSFs address.

One object, multiple degrees of freedom

An arch remains an arch as attention moves from its base to its crown. A tree remains a tree across species, size, and foliage. These examples motivate a distinction between concept presence and coordinates within a concept. A single scalar can report presence or strength, but cannot generally distinguish several independent variations. The source's DINOv3 examples associate positions within a learned block with different parts of an arch or a hand. Its SDXL examples explore variation within generated hats, coffee, and pretzels.

A subspace is not itself a manifold. A subspace is closed under arbitrary linear combinations; a manifold is a set that locally admits a low-dimensional coordinate chart. A curved manifold may lie inside a larger linear subspace. BSFs discover the enclosing blocks; the geometry of activations observed inside a block requires further analysis. This prevents a seductive but incorrect inference that every point in a learned block is a valid concept instance.

What changes in the objective

Let $x\in\mathbb R^d$ be an activation and let block $b$ have coordinates $z_b\in\mathbb R^{r_b}$ and decoder $D_b\in\mathbb R^{d\times r_b}$. A block reconstruction is $\hat x=c+\sum_{b=1}^{B}D_bz_b$, where $c$ is a bias and $B$ is the number of candidate blocks. A representative structured-sparsity objective is

$$\mathbb E\left[\|x-\hat x\|_2^2+\lambda\sum_{b=1}^{B}\|z_b\|_2\right].$$

This illustrates the group-lasso principle, rather than claiming all BSF variants use this exact penalty. The source also describes vanilla and Grassmannian variants. Its vanilla encoder retains blocks by their Euclidean norms using block TopK. Within a selected block, several coordinates can remain nonzero without each paying a separate selection cost.

Why does the norm matter? If $Q$ is an orthogonal $r_b\times r_b$ matrix, replace $z_b$ by $Qz_b$ and $D_b$ by $D_bQ^\top$. Reconstruction is unchanged because $Q^\top Q=I$, and $\|Qz_b\|_2=\|z_b\|_2$. A block can therefore express its concept independently of an arbitrary orthogonal choice of internal axes. Coordinatewise $\ell_1$ sparsity generally lacks this invariance. This does not establish identifiability across blocks; correlated concepts can still admit competing decompositions.

Original worked example: rotating curves

This is a teaching construction, not a reproduction of the source experiments. Encode orientation $\theta$ by $x(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,\cos2\theta,\sin2\theta)$. A single four-dimensional block reconstructs this curve exactly. Two harmonics change at different rates: after a half-turn, the first pair changes sign while the second pair returns to its starting point. Thus a neuron reading only the second pair can respond with period $\pi$ even though the object traverses a full $2\pi$ cycle.

For uniformly distributed $\theta$, the mean is zero and covariance is $C=I_4/2$, by trigonometric orthogonality. Its participation ratio, defined here as $(\operatorname{tr}C)^2/\operatorname{tr}(C^2)$, is $4$. Yet the data trace a one-dimensional curve. Effective linear dimension and intrinsic manifold dimension measure different properties. The source reports higher harmonics in InceptionV1 curve representations; this calculation explains the mathematical possibility without establishing the network's actual coefficients.

Evidence and scope

The synthetic recovery test has known ground-truth manifolds, so recovery can be assessed directly. Real-model blocks have no such ground truth: reconstruction, description length, visual coherence, and steering provide complementary evidence. Better reconstruction alone could reflect greater effective capacity. Fair comparisons must account for block size, active coordinates, training budget, and coding cost. A successful image edit establishes an effect of that intervention; it does not establish that every block has a unique human concept.

The source discusses stable rank as an effective-dimensionality diagnostic and explicitly treats its vision conclusions as provisional. A low effective rank does not prove a particular topology or intrinsic dimension. Domain-specific priors may be needed for temporal or language representations.

Exercises

  1. Conceptual: Why can a block recover a coherent arch concept while its individual axes lack stable semantic labels?
  2. Mathematical: For $x(\theta)=(2\cos\theta,2\sin\theta,\cos2\theta,\sin2\theta)$, compute the covariance participation ratio.
  3. Critical experiment: Design a comparison that tests whether BSFs recover geometry better than an SAE without rewarding extra active coordinates.
