A detector must act before the tool call

A cyber agent can inspect the same vulnerability for legitimate repair or malicious exploitation. The relevant distinction therefore includes authorization, intent, and the preceding trajectory. This October 2026 production report asks whether internal activation classifiers can screen every turn cheaply enough to support synchronous intervention. Its argument combines a statistical claim about selecting suspicious turns with a systems claim about preserving inference throughput. Neither claim alone establishes a deployable monitor.

Prerequisites: classification and decision thresholds, experimental and systems literacy, and the introductory probe-monitor guide. An activation is an intermediate numerical representation computed by the target network. A probe is a supervised function mapping those representations to a score. A cascade first applies this cheap score and sends selected turns to a more expensive judge.

Original teaching case: cascade arithmetic

Let $Y=1$ denote a harmful turn, with prevalence $\pi=P(Y=1)$. Let $A$ mean escalation by the probe and $B$ mean rejection by the judge. Define probe recall $r=P(A\mid Y=1)$ and false-positive rate $f=P(A\mid Y=0)$. The escalation fraction follows from total probability:

$$q=P(A)=\pi r+(1-\pi)f.$$

If the probe costs $c_p$ and one judge call costs $c_j$, expected monitoring cost is $c_p+qc_j$. The all-judge comparator costs $c_j$. Cascade recall is

$$P(A\cap B\mid Y=1)=rP(B\mid A,Y=1).$$

The conditional judge term matters: using its unconditional recall assumes away selection effects. For a hypothetical prevalence $\pi=0.01$, $r=0.99$, and $f=0.01$, the judge sees $q=0.0198$ of turns. With negligible probe cost this yields about $50.5$ times fewer calls. If conditional judge recall is $0.95$, overall recall becomes $0.9405$. These invented teaching inputs illustrate why excellent first-stage recall and workload reduction can coexist; they are not a reconstruction of the source's operating point.

A turn-level false-positive rate also differs sharply from the source's session-level interruption budget. Under the deliberately simplistic assumption of independent benign turns with identical false-positive probability $f$, a session of $n$ turns is interrupted with probability $1-(1-f)^n$. At $f=0.001$ and $n=100$, this is about $9.52\%$. Correlation changes that calculation, so production calibration must retain complete sessions rather than shuffle individual turns.

Read the measured claims at their actual unit

In “Evaluating Our Monitors,” the authors describe approximately 2,400 sessions and more than 60,000 turns, using Opus 5 labels as the reference. They report approximately 93% harmful-session recall at 5.5% benign-session interruption. “Inference Optimizations” describes custom kernels and preserving probe histories through serving features including prefix reuse and speculative decoding. Throughput changes below 2% were within benchmark noise. This supports no measurable throughput penalty on the tested workloads, rather than a universal zero-overhead theorem.

The cost comparisons use configurations satisfying a recall and interruption constraint. The reported USD 65 and USD 185 judge costs per million turns belong to non-reasoning and reasoning cascade configurations. They should not be combined with another figure's operating point without checking selection rules. “External Red Teaming Results” describes a preliminary, two-day, non-adaptive static attack portfolio. Zero successful universal strategies in that battery coexists with successful individual interactions. It does not mean the system cannot be bypassed.

The probe's signal demonstrates useful decodability and monitoring performance. It does not by itself explain the causal mechanism of cyber intent. Important alternatives include learning dataset style, matching the reference judge's preferences, or exploiting simulation artifacts. Strong follow-up tests would hold out complete task families and user simulators, use independent expert adjudication, and evaluate adaptive attacks against the deployed intervention policy. The architecture must also enforce a barrier before tool execution; fast detection after an action is still retrospective.

Reading route and capability

Read “Our Monitor Recipe,” “Architecture: a monitor cascade,” “Inference Optimizations,” “Evaluating Our Monitors,” and “External Red Teaming Results,” then the appendix comparison of on-policy and off-policy monitors. You should be able to distinguish probe calibration, cascade recall, benign-session interruption, inference throughput, and action-blocking latency. Explore the local experiments as scaled illustrations.

Exercises

  1. Why can a cascade empirically outperform its judge-only baseline without establishing that discarding information is intrinsically beneficial?
  2. For $\pi=0.02$, $r=0.98$, $f=0.03$, and conditional judge recall $0.96$, compute escalation and overall recall.
  3. Design a test of the claim that monitoring prevents harmful tool execution.
