Can we take apart the computation itself?

Suppose an activation feature detects a closing bracket. That tells us something about a state of the network, but leaves a mechanistic question unanswered: which parameter operations detected the opening bracket, transported its identity, and caused the closing token? The May 5, 2026 paper develops adVersarial Parameter Decomposition (VPD) to study those operations directly. It decomposes weight matrices into simple pieces while retaining the original architecture and nonlinearities. Optional preparation: linear algebra, transformer computation, and representations and geometry. The shorter publisher summary is a complementary entry point.

The target is a 67-million-parameter language model. The paper studies attention algorithms, attribution graphs for particular predictions, and a direct emoticon edit. Its central achievement is an experimentally useful decomposition method. It does not supply a complete executable human explanation of this model, prove unique recovery of true mechanisms, or establish scaling to frontier models.

Parameters, subcomponents, and residuals

Let $f(x;\theta)$ be a fixed network with input $x$ and parameters $\theta$. For a decomposed matrix $W_l\in\mathbb R^{a_l\times b_l}$ at layer or matrix index $l$, write a rank-one subcomponent as $A_{lc}=u_{lc}v_{lc}^{\top}$, where $u_{lc}\in\mathbb R^{a_l}$ is its output direction and $v_{lc}\in\mathbb R^{b_l}$ its input direction. The subcomponent reads the scalar $v_{lc}^{\top}h$ from an input activation $h$ and writes $u_{lc}(v_{lc}^{\top}h)$. This is a concrete linear computation; a semantic label is a subsequent interpretation.

VPD allows more subcomponents than the matrix rank. Unlike singular value decomposition, the objective depends on their downstream effects through the network's nonlinear computation, and neither orthogonality nor an energy-ordered basis is the goal. Define the residual matrix $\Delta_l=W_l-\sum_c A_{lc}$. Then

$$W_l=\sum_c A_{lc}+\Delta_l$$

holds exactly by construction. This is parameter faithfulness. It is an accounting identity that includes a potentially complicated residual. If the learned rank-one terms alone miss important computation, adding the exact residual restores the original matrix without making that computation interpretable. A small residual penalty and tests that ablate the residual address this loophole; the identity alone cannot.

A subcomponent occupies one matrix and is zero elsewhere in the full parameter vector. A full component may combine subcomponents across matrices. The paper clusters them after training using patterns of causal importance and a description-length criterion. Thus a displayed rank-one subcomponent is not necessarily a self-contained algorithm or even the complete unit the authors ultimately wish to identify.

Four demands that should not be conflated

DemandOperational meaningWhat it does not establish
Parameter faithfulnessParts plus residual sum to original weights.Each part is semantically meaningful.
MinimalityFew parts are necessary on a particular input.The retained parts form the unique true explanation.
Mechanistic faithfulnessParts deemed unimportant tolerate many allowed ablations without changing output.Finite adversarial search has checked every mask.
SimplicityParts use little machinery and specialize across data.Rank one implies one human concept.

For batch item $b$, token position $t$, and subcomponent $c$, a learned auxiliary network predicts causal importance $g_{btlc}\in[0,1]$. A mask $m_{btlc}\in[g_{btlc},1]$ multiplies the corresponding parameter subcomponent during that forward pass. Importance one forces retention; importance zero permits complete retention, complete removal, or anything between. Intermediate values restrict how far the component may be reduced. Residual components have importance zero by definition and are also exposed to ablation.

The ideal criterion requires output preservation for every allowed mask combination, including leaving arbitrary unimportant components on. This is stricter than removing all of them together. To see why, imagine two operations contributing $+3$ and $-3$ to an output. Removing both preserves that output, but removing only one changes it. The joint-ablation test can mislabel both as irrelevant by exploiting cancellation. A box of independently varied masks exposes the dependence.

Deriving the role of all five loss terms

Let $B$ be batch size, $T$ sequence length, $L$ the number of decomposed matrices, and $C_l$ the number of rank-one terms in matrix $l$. Let $P_{bt}$ be the original next-token distribution and $Q_{bt}(m)$ the masked network's distribution. For clarity, use a normalized token average

$$D(m)=\frac1{BT}\sum_{b,t}\sum_j P_{bt,j}\log\frac{P_{bt,j}}{Q_{bt,j}(m)},$$

where $j$ indexes vocabulary tokens. This is a pedagogical token-level spelling of the reconstruction criterion; reduction conventions can move factors into loss weights. Kullback–Leibler divergence is zero exactly when the two positive distributions agree. The method uses a stochastic reconstruction term $L_{\rm stoch}=\mathbb E_m D(m)$ under sampled allowed masks and an adversarial term $L_{\rm adv}=D(\hat m)$, where $\hat m$ is found by gradient ascent with projection back into the allowed mask box.

The adversarial term targets failures that random masks rarely visit. Its search maximum is a lower bound on the true worst case: failing to find a destructive mask does not certify its absence. Gradients can be uninformative, the search can be too short, or the mask parameterization can miss important structure. Increasing attack strength at evaluation is therefore a substantive scientific test, not just a numerical detail.

Choose a positive exponent $p$. The importance-minimality term is

$$L_{\rm imp}=\frac1{BT}\sum_{b,t,l,c}g_{btlc}^{p}.$$

It favors small causal-importance budgets. With reconstruction losses alone, declaring every component important would eliminate most ablation freedom and make faithfulness easy. Importance minimization prevents that vacuous answer. Conversely, minimizing importance alone would declare everything removable. The tradeoff has to be assessed as a reconstruction-versus-complexity frontier.

Minimality per input does not guarantee specialization across inputs. One rank-one term can implement several tasks on disjoint contexts, for example when its input direction has different uses at positive and negative projection values. Define the batch frequency-like quantity $s_{lc}=\sum_{b,t}g_{btlc}^{p}$. The source's frequency-minimality penalty can be rewritten compactly as

$$L_{\rm freq}=\frac1{BT}\sum_{l,c}s_{lc}\log_2(1+s_{lc}).$$

Because this penalty is superlinear in total importance mass, splitting a widely used part into less frequent specialized parts can reduce it even when the total per-input importance budget is unchanged. This encourages simplicity through specialization. It is a chosen inductive bias, not a proof that one feature corresponds to one mechanism. Its batch dependence and interaction with other terms matter when comparing implementations.

Finally, with $N$ decomposable scalar parameters, $L_\Delta=N^{-1}\sum_l\|\Delta_l\|_F^2$ discourages hiding computation in the unrestricted residual. The Frobenius norm $\|A\|_F^2$ is the sum of squared entries of matrix $A$. The total objective is

$$L=\beta_1L_{\rm adv}+\beta_2L_{\rm stoch}+\beta_3L_{\rm imp}+\beta_4L_{\rm freq}+\beta_5L_\Delta,$$

where the nonnegative $\beta_i$ are hyperparameters, not probabilities or universal constants. Each term closes a different loophole. Decreasing any single scalar loss cannot by itself establish a better decomposition; an optimizer can sacrifice another desired property. The paper's training recipe is relevant precisely because these tradeoffs require tuning.

Original worked example: exact weights, unfaithful story

Consider the invented linear map $W=I_2$, the two-dimensional identity, and input $x=(1,0)^\top$. Decompose it into $A_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $A_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, $A_3=-A_2$, and $A_4=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. All four matrices have rank one and their sum is exactly $W$, with zero residual. At this input, $A_2x=A_3x=A_4x=0$, so they really can be independently ablated.

Change only the example to $x=(0,1)^\top$. Now $A_2x=(1,0)^\top$, $A_3x=(-1,0)^\top$, and $A_4x=(0,1)^\top$. Removing $A_2$ and $A_3$ together leaves the correct output $(0,1)^\top$. Removing only $A_2$ gives $(-1,1)^\top$, with squared output error one. Parameter faithfulness and joint reconstruction were perfect, but a claim that both canceling terms were independently irrelevant was false.

For independent uniform masks on the canceling pair, the first output coordinate is $m_2-m_3$. Its expected squared error is $\operatorname{Var}(m_2)+\operatorname{Var}(m_3)=1/6$, while the worst-case squared error is one at a corner of the unit square. This calculation shows exactly what stochastic and adversarial testing ask. It uses a squared-error toy output, not the paper's language-model KL objective, and is not a replication of VPD training.

The frequency term has a different job. If a component is fully important on eight tokens, its unnormalized frequency cost is $8\log_2 9$. Two specialized components, each important on four disjoint tokens, cost $8\log_2 5$. Total importance remains eight, but the frequency cost falls. Neither construction says the split is valid: the reconstruction and adversarial tests must still pass.

Why attention requires pairs of parts

For column activation vectors $h_t,h_s\in\mathbb R^d$, query and key matrices for attention head $a$ give a pre-softmax score $h_t^\top W_{Q,a}^\top W_{K,a}h_s$. The usual attention scale and causal mask can be applied afterward. Substitute $W_{Q,a}=\sum_c u^a_{Q,c}v_{Q,c}^\top$ and its key analogue. Distributing the product yields

$$h_t^\top W_{Q,a}^\top W_{K,a}h_s=\sum_{c,c'}(v_{Q,c}^\top h_t)\big((u^a_{Q,c})^\top u^a_{K,c'}\big)(v_{K,c'}^\top h_s).$$

Each term has a query activation, a static query/key coupling, and a key activation. Large static coupling alone does not imply use on an example: either activation factor can be zero. The source finds previous-token and syntax-boundary behaviors involving subcomponents distributed across heads. Head boundaries are therefore not guaranteed boundaries of the recovered functional units.

After the score, softmax couples all candidate source positions. The value/output pathway then determines what information is transported. A QK interaction identifies a routing preference, not the content ultimately written into the residual stream. To explain a behavior, one needs both routing and transported content, followed by downstream computation. The paper's worked attention cases supply evidence for some such pathways, without exhausting all attention behavior.

What the results support, and where they fail

In “The decomposition model behaves similarly to the target model,” the target validation cross-entropy is 2.71, compared with 2.72 for unmasked subcomponents without residuals and 2.84 under stochastic masks. The near match in the first comparison is substantially weaker than robust agreement under masks. The same section reports adversarial KL of 0.8280 at 20 optimization steps, 25.2560 at 160, and 40.2200 at 320. The stronger attacks find combinations labeled permissible that badly alter behavior. Finite adversarial robustness is an achieved degree, not an established universal quantifier.

The authors note that arbitrarily strong robustness may not even be the right target: independently perturbing rank-one terms can be stricter than perturbing their coherent clusters. This is a question about the appropriate intervention family. It does not erase the observed failure at stronger attacks. A reviewer should ask which mask combinations correspond to plausible removal of mechanisms and how changing that family changes the interpretation.

The emoticon edit changes a selected output direction toward the unembedding vector for “o,” demonstrating a concrete connection from a parameter part to output behavior. Off-target comparisons are encouraging but do not imply arbitrary semantic edits. Similarly, the pronoun and bracket-closing attribution graphs support local information-flow hypotheses. The paper explicitly distinguishes an attribution graph from a computational graph: edge weights summarize influence but do not contain the functions required to run the algorithm independently. Saturated attention can make local gradients understate large intervention effects.

VPD builds on stochastic parameter decomposition (SPD), adding adversarial sampling and frequency minimality among other changes. It is not simply a new name for SPD. Nor is it an SAE or transcoder: those fit activation representations or activation transformations, whereas VPD decomposes original parameters and executes their original nonlinear architecture. None of these methodological distinctions makes comparative performance universal across models, cost budgets, sparsity definitions, or tasks.

Reading locators and explanatory target

Use the local publisher Markdown for “The core method,” all five subsections through “Summary of loss terms,” “The decomposition model behaves similarly to the target model,” “The QK circuit consists of interactions between pairs of parameter subcomponents,” “Pruning for specific behaviors,” and “Other limitations and future work.” The appendix's “Delta-L2 penalty” and “Training recipe for VPD” clarify implementation. This guide covers the method and selected evidence; it does not reproduce every annotated circuit or appendix experiment. Be able to derive the QK pair interaction, explain each loss's loophole, and state exactly why the strongest mechanistic claim remains unproved.

Exercises

  1. Concept. Why does a zero residual fail to establish mechanistic faithfulness? Give a counterexample and explain what an attribution graph still omits.
  2. Math. In the canceling-pair example, let both causal importances equal $a\in[0,1]$, so each mask is uniform on $[a,1]$. Compute expected and worst-case squared output errors. What happens at $a=1$?
  3. Experiment. Propose a small local evaluation of a frozen decomposition that could distinguish genuine robustness from weak adversarial search. Include how you would interpret a newly discovered failure.
