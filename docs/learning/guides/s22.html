From a variant score to a biological hypothesis

A pathogenicity classifier can rank genetic variants without explaining which biological process might be disrupted. This April 2026 research summary proposes a two-part approach: predict pathogenicity from Evo 2 representations, then read annotation changes and synthesize a candidate explanation. The important distinction is between accurate ranking, a biologically meaningful disruption profile, and a clinically validated interpretation. The source explicitly describes computational predictions as hypotheses.

Prerequisites: frozen representations and probes, validation and calibration, and second-order sequence pooling. A reference sequence is the baseline DNA sequence; a variant sequence contains the change being assessed. Let $H_r,H_v\in\mathbb R^{T\times d}$ denote their aligned representations in a simplified equal-length case. A probe is a learned supervised map over these representations. Insertions and deletions require additional alignment choices beyond this simplified notation.

Original teaching model: prediction and explanation can diverge

Let $a_j(H)\in[0,1]$ predict biological annotation $j$, such as membership in a known functional region. Define a disruption coordinate $\delta_j=a_j(H_v)-a_j(H_r)$. The vector $\delta$ summarizes changes in predicted annotation scores. If $a_j$ is logistic with logit $w_j^\top h+b_j$, a small embedding shift $\Delta h$ gives the first-order approximation

$$\delta_j\approx a_j(h_r)(1-a_j(h_r))w_j^\top\Delta h.$$

This follows by differentiating the logistic function. The same embedding displacement produces a larger probability change near a baseline of $0.5$ than near zero or one. Thus disruption magnitudes depend on probe calibration and saturation, not only biological magnitude.

For an invented exact example, an annotation logit moves from $2$ to $0$. Its probability falls from $\sigma(2)\approx0.8808$ to $0.5$, so $\delta\approx-0.3808$. This is a change in a model's predicted annotation. It does not prove a 38.08% reduction in molecular activity. A language model can verbalize that change coherently while inheriting all errors of the upstream probe.

AUROC introduces a separate issue. It equals the probability that a randomly drawn positive receives a higher score than a randomly drawn negative, with standard tie handling. It does not measure calibration. Any strictly increasing transformation preserves AUROC while potentially changing numerical scores drastically. Hence a high AUROC cannot make a score a disease probability or determine a clinical action threshold.

What the release actually contributes

“Probing for pathogenicity” reports an overall AUROC of 0.997 on 839,000 ClinVar single-nucleotide variants and zero-shot short-indel performance of 0.991. “Explaining 4.2 million variants” describes annotation probes on reference embeddings, comparison of predicted functions before and after a variant, and reasoning-model synthesis with metadata. These are distinct stages with different supervision and failure modes.

The explanation evaluation uses an LLM judge on 154 high-confidence ClinVar variants and reports a composite score improvement over metadata alone. This is evidence that the generated explanations better match the chosen rubric and references. It is not an independent wet-lab demonstration that every named mechanism is correct. Natural-language fluency can conceal an unsupported causal step between an annotation change and a disease phenotype.

Relevant confounders include related variants or genes crossing data splits, label ascertainment, database review practices, and overlap between explanatory metadata and evaluation references. No single overall score resolves performance on uncertain variants, underrepresented ancestries, new genes, or discordant laboratory evidence. A claim of generalization should name the held-out unit: variant, genomic region, gene, time, or assay.

“What this points toward” explicitly notes the need for experimental validation, the inability of annotation probes to surface entirely unknown mechanisms, and the preprint status of the research at publication. The resource's scale measures coverage of computed outputs. It does not imply that millions of previously uncertain variants now have clinically established effects. This companion is about evaluating scientific inference, not making health decisions.

Reading route and capability

Read “Background: Evo 2,” “Probing for pathogenicity,” “Explaining 4.2 million variants,” and the limitation bullets in “What this points toward.” You should draw the pipeline from sequence to frozen representations, pathogenicity score, disruption profile, and generated hypothesis, identifying the validation required at every arrow. Compare Evo 2's SAE study for unsupervised features rather than predefined annotations.

Exercises

  1. Why does an annotation probe's explanation cover only known mechanisms?
  2. An annotation logit shifts from $0$ to $-\log3$. Compute the probability change and interpret it correctly.
  3. Design a validation split that tests transfer to new genes rather than neighboring variants.
