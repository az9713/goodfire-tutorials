Can removable parameter pieces reveal a computation?

“Towards Scalable Parameter Decomposition” introduces Stochastic Parameter Decomposition, or SPD. Its question is whether a model's weights can be split into simple components whose input-dependent necessity is tested by removal. This is a distinct method from the later VPD work in source 5. The present source compares SPD primarily with Attribution-based Parameter Decomposition, or APD, and reports toy-model results.

Prerequisites: rank-one matrices, neural-network functions, and interventions. A parameter decomposition writes a weight matrix $W\in\mathbb R^{d_o\times d_i}$ as a sum of components $W=\sum_{k=1}^K u_kv_k^\top$, where $u_k\in\mathbb R^{d_o}$ and $v_k\in\mathbb R^{d_i}$. Each outer product has rank at most one. Faithfulness concerns reconstructing the target behavior; minimality concerns using few components per input; simplicity constrains component form or size.

Original teaching model: masking is an intervention

For a linear map $y=Wx$, introduce component-retention variables $m_k\in\{0,1\}$ and masked output $\tilde y=\sum_k m_ku_kv_k^\top x$. The contribution removed with component $k$ is $a_k=u_k(v_k^\top x)$. If independent masks retain components with probabilities $r_k$, the expected squared output distortion is

$$E\|y-\tilde y\|^2=\left\|\sum_k(1-r_k)a_k\right\|^2+\sum_kr_k(1-r_k)\|a_k\|^2.$$

To derive it, write the removed sum as its mean plus a zero-mean random residual. The squared norm's cross term has expectation zero. Independence eliminates cross-covariances in the residual, leaving the Bernoulli variances. This equation is an original simplified masking model, not the complete SPD loss or mask distribution.

It reveals why component interactions matter. For $a_1=v$ and $a_2=-v$, removing both leaves no output distortion, while removing only one does. Independent masks expose this cancellation through the variance term. A gradient-based importance estimate at the intact output can miss such context-dependent effects. Conversely, successful removal only establishes dispensability relative to the tested inputs, components, and intervention distribution.

In a concrete teaching example, $W=I_2=e_1e_1^\top+e_2e_2^\top$, with coordinate basis vectors $e_1,e_2$. At input $x=(3,0)$, the second component contributes zero and can be removed exactly; the first cannot. At $x=(0,4)$ their roles reverse. This is input-dependent necessity, even though both components are needed to implement the map on all inputs.

The source's advance and its boundary

“Our new method: Stochastic Parameter Decomposition” describes rank-one components and learned causal-importance functions. “Technical details: How stochastic masking works” explains learning through sampled ablation patterns instead of relying only on gradient attribution. The blog gives an overview and points to the paper for the full algorithm. Its exact optimization coefficients and detailed masking distribution should not be reconstructed from the analogy alone.

“What we found” reports recovery in known-ground-truth toy systems, including superposition and multilayer models, with improved robustness relative to APD. The illustrated five-feature/two-dimensional example uses twenty available components, with excess components shrinking toward zero. Ground truth is an advantage for method validation, but it also constrains what success means: known synthetic mechanisms may be much simpler than distributed language-model computations.

“Looking forward” explicitly acknowledges nontrivial computational cost, remaining sensitivity, and the need to cluster rank-one pieces into full mechanisms. A rank-one factorization is not intrinsically unique: rescaling $u_k$ and inversely rescaling $v_k$ preserves its matrix, while alternative sums can implement the same function. Structural constraints and intervention evidence reduce ambiguity without guaranteeing a single canonical decomposition.

SPD addresses decomposition first. Interpretation and validation remain further stages. A component that survives a mask can be necessary under that intervention while its human label remains wrong. A complete mechanistic claim must test the proposed role across counterfactual inputs and distinguish competing decompositions with comparable behavioral fidelity.

Reading route and capability

Read “Why focus on parameters?,” “Parameter decomposition,” both SPD method sections, “What we found,” and “Looking forward.” You should explain the distinction between reconstructing weights, preserving behavior, input-specific masking, and naming a mechanism. Compare SPD with VPD only after studying each method's own objective and evidence.

Exercises

  1. Why does minimality on a dataset not imply a globally minimal mechanism?
  2. For $a_1=(2,0)$, $a_2=(0,3)$, and independent retention probabilities $r_1=r_2=1/2$, compute expected squared distortion.
  3. Design a test for redundant or canceling decomposition components.
