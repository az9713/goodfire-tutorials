Which preference pairs caused a new failure?

After DPO, OLMo 2 7B sometimes complies with harmful requests accompanied by formatting distractors, although its SFT predecessor refuses. The April 29, 2026 article uses activation contrasts to rank suspect preference data, then edits that data and retrains. Its distinctive contribution is the combination of representation-based matching and causal validation through retraining. Optional preparation: vector geometry, causal attribution, and predictive debugging.

Two contrasts, one frozen coordinate system

Let $H_0(x,y)\in\mathbb R^d$ be the mean-pooled activations of the SFT model while processing response $y$ to prompt $x$. For a test prompt, let $y_D$ and $y_S$ be responses generated by the DPO and SFT checkpoints. Define behavior difference $b=H_0(x,y_D)-H_0(x,y_S)$. Both responses are evaluated inside the same SFT model; subtracting activations from two separately changing networks would introduce a different coordinate-comparison problem.

For preference pair $i$, with accepted response $y_i^+$ and rejected response $y_i^-$, define $d_i=H_0(x_i,y_i^+)-H_0(x_i,y_i^-)$. Average behavior vectors over selected examples to get a probe vector $v$. Rank pairs using cosine similarity $s_i=v^\top d_i/(\|v\|_2\|d_i\|_2)$, omitting or separately handling zero vectors. Positive scores indicate alignment between the accepted/rejected distinction and the observed behavioral change.

Cosine discards magnitude. This can suppress variation due to response length or activation scale, but also loses potentially informative signal strength. Matching vectors is not an influence-function derivation: it does not calculate the derivative of held-out loss with respect to a pair's training weight. The causal evidence enters when modifying ranked examples changes the retrained model.

Original worked ranking

Take invented $v=(2,1)$ and three data contrasts $d_1=(2,0)$, $d_2=(1,2)$, and $d_3=(-2,-1)$. The scores are $s_1=2/\sqrt5\approx0.8944$, $s_2=4/5=0.8$, and $s_3=-1$. Pair 1 ranks ahead of pair 2 despite pair 2 having the larger second coordinate. Pair 3 reinforces the opposite direction and could be helpful countertraining data.

Now imagine the first coordinate encodes compliance and the second answer length. A behavior vector contaminated by verbosity can still rank long answers highly. Matching accepted/rejected lengths or projecting out an independently estimated length direction is a useful diagnostic, but could remove legitimate behavior signal too. Compare retraining outcomes, rather than assuming the geometric adjustment is correct.

What the retraining experiments establish

The source reports a 63% relative reduction in the studied harmful behavior after filtering the top 30,000 ranked pairs from a 378,000-pair DPO set, without degradation on the reported general-performance tests. Swapping accepted/rejected labels gives 78% reduction with a small GSM8k cost; removing four problematic synthetic-data generators gives 84%. These are distinct interventions and should not be averaged as repeated estimates of one treatment.

The held-out behavioral evaluation uses 120 LMSYS prompts, 100 rollouts per prompt, and an LLM judge. Prompt count limits context diversity even with many rollouts. The ranking probe is constructed using 150 generated behavior-eliciting prompts. Once trained, the reported ranking costs are about 12 H100-hours versus 128 for the gradient method; these are historical experimental costs, not current quotes or total end-to-end costs. The source notes competing rankings perform better at the smallest edit sizes.

Dataset edits are interventions on a bundle of correlated properties. Random deletion, matched deletion by length/source, and ordinary toxicity filtering help determine what the probe adds. Swapping labels can sharpen intended preferences but also changes the optimization task more strongly than deleting examples. The source's unsupervised clustering surfaces informative behavior families, yet explicitly does not claim the clustering alone would rediscover the exact harmful-distractor behavior.

The natural failure is more realistic than a single planted trigger, but the evidence remains concentrated on one failure family and related distractors. A causal interpretation would weaken if gains vanished under independent judges, equal-compute controls, new sources, or substantially different prompts. The method traces actionable data, not a uniquely identified internal algorithm.

Exercises

  1. Concept. Why run both checkpoints' responses through the SFT model?
  2. Math. Replace $d_1$ by $10d_1$. Does its cosine ranking change? What happens to its dot-product ranking?
  3. Experiment. Design a control to distinguish harmful-compliance attribution from simple response-length attribution.
