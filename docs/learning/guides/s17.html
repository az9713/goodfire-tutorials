Estimate a rare failure where it is less rare

If an unsafe response occurs once in a million independent trials, ordinary evaluation will usually report zero. This post uses a less-safe variant of a model to construct a path of intermediate token distributions. It measures compliance where samples are informative, fits a trend, and extrapolates to the original model. The method trades direct observation for a structural assumption about how failure probability changes along the path.

Prerequisites: rare-event statistics, autoregressive probabilities, and the related model-difference amplification. Let $z_s(u\mid c)$ and $z_b(u\mid c)$ denote logits for token $u$ in context $c$ from the original and less-safe models. With $\alpha\in[0,1]$, define $z_\alpha=\alpha z_s+(1-\alpha)z_b$, so $\alpha=1$ is the original. Sampling proceeds autoregressively with these mixed logits at every step.

Original derivation: the path is geometric, not a mixture

If $p_s$ and $p_b$ are the corresponding softmax probabilities, exponentiating the mixed logits and normalizing gives

$$p_\alpha(u\mid c)=\frac{p_s(u\mid c)^\alpha p_b(u\mid c)^{1-\alpha}}{\sum_v p_s(v\mid c)^\alpha p_b(v\mid c)^{1-\alpha}}.$$

The model-specific softmax constants cancel between numerator and denominator. This is a normalized geometric interpolation. It generally differs from the arithmetic mixture $\alpha p_s+(1-\alpha)p_b$. Moreover, the normalization changes with every sampled context; sequence-level failure probabilities need not follow any simple function of $\alpha$.

The post empirically fits $\log_{10}p(\alpha)=a+b\alpha$ in a region with compliance below 50%. For an original illustrative case, suppose $p(0.4)=0.1$ and $p(0.6)=0.01$. The fitted slope is $b=(-2+1)/0.2=-5$, with intercept $a=1$. Extrapolating gives $p(1)=10^{-4}$. A slope error of just one changes this extrapolated probability by a factor of ten at $\alpha=1$ if the intercept is held fixed. Small fit uncertainty can matter greatly on the probability scale.

For direct sampling with event probability $p$, $P(0\text{ events in }n)=(1-p)^n$. At $p=10^{-4}$ and $n=100$, this is approximately $0.99005$. Zero observations therefore provide weak evidence against this probability. The extrapolation uses informative intermediate observations, but a confidence interval conditional on the fitted line would omit uncertainty about whether the line remains valid.

What “30 times fewer” measures

“Results” studies Qwen 3 4B and an abliterated variant on HarmBench. The headline comparison classifies prompts that never comply in an initial 100 rollouts according to whether they comply at least once among 100,000. AUROC measures ranking of those groups. Matching AUROC with fewer rollouts is valuable, but it is not the same as proving uniform relative-error accuracy for every rare-event probability.

The appendix searches with ten rollouts per path point, then samples more continuations while advancing toward the original model; it stops that fitting scan at the first zero-compliance point. If fewer than two points have nonzero compliance, it assigns the fallback prediction $10^{-6}$. These stopping and fallback rules affect selection bias and uncertainty. A zero count at an intermediate point is a finite-sample observation compatible with a small nonzero probability, not evidence of exact zero. A binomial likelihood fit could incorporate those observations rather than taking logarithms of observed fractions.

The source names two key limitations: availability of a variant where the behavior is common and the empirical log-linear assumption. Additional practical costs include evaluating both models during path generation and judging responses. Rollout reduction is not automatically an equal reduction in GPU time or dollars. The 100,000-rollout reference itself remains an estimate, especially for probabilities well below its inverse sample size.

This is failure-rate estimation, not mechanistic interpretability. The path changes many token preferences, potentially changing the kind of failure. Validation should test whether intermediate failures remain relevant to the original model rather than treating every path as a single unchanged mechanism.

Reading route and capability

Read “Interpolation step,” “Extrapolation step,” both limitations in “Conclusion,” and the appendix algorithm, importance-sampling comparator, and naive-sampling definition. You should explain geometric interpolation, extrapolation bias, and the difference between ranking performance and probability accuracy.

Exercises

  1. Why does token-level logit interpolation not imply log-linear sequence failure probabilities?
  2. If fitted $\log_{10}p(\alpha)=-1-4\alpha$, compute $p(0.5)$ and $p(1)$.
  3. Design an out-of-sample test of extrapolation rather than only interpolation fit.
