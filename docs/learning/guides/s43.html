What would count as progress in interpretability?

This review asks which conceptual, technical, application, and social problems stand between readable model fragments and useful mechanistic understanding. It is a synthesis of research perspectives, not an experimental demonstration that any single method solves those problems. Its dated field assessment should be read alongside the collection's later studies.

Preparation: model components, causal validation, evaluation and uncertainty, decomposition methods. Use this companion to evaluate claims throughout the course.

Decompose, describe, validate

The review separates choosing network components from explaining their functional roles. Native architectural units, low-dimensional projections, and sparse dictionary features offer different decompositions. A decomposition can be convenient without aligning with the system's generalization structure. A readable label can also be incomplete or false.

Description has at least two directions: what causes a component to activate and what its activation changes downstream. Validation asks whether the description predicts new observations, interventions, unusual failures, or the behavior of a handcrafted replacement. A method that explains its own chosen examples has not yet cleared that bar.

The review also distinguishes reverse engineering, which discovers candidate roles from the network, from concept-based analysis, which begins with an investigator-defined property. Both are useful. The latter can miss unexpected structure, while the former can generate stories that need substantial testing. Automation inherits these issues; scaling the number of descriptions does not automatically improve their fidelity.

Original worked example: information without causal use

Let input contain binary property $c$ and another scalar $x$. A network's hidden state is $h=(c,x)$, but its output is $y=x$. A linear probe $\hat c=(1,0)^\top h$ recovers $c$ perfectly. Yet intervening on $h_1$ while holding $h_2$ fixed changes $y$ by zero. The property is present in the representation and unused by this output computation.

This is an exact teaching counterexample, not a reported model from the review. It proves that decodability alone does not imply causal mediation. It does not prove probes are useless: a correlated signal may be an effective monitor if it generalizes, even when it is not the mechanism producing the behavior.

Now let $y=x+\epsilon c$ for small nonzero $\epsilon$. The property has a causal effect, but it may not matter for a thresholded decision unless the margin is small. Causal use, practical importance, and sufficient explanation are therefore different standards. An evaluation must say which one it targets.

Why average success cannot certify complete coverage

Suppose an interpretability audit examines $N$ independent cases and misses a failure type occurring with probability $p$. The miss probability is $(1-p)^N$. For a desired miss probability at most $\delta\in(0,1)$, detection by sampling requires

$$N\geq\frac{\log\delta}{\log(1-p)}\approx\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{p}\quad\text{for small }p.$$

The logarithms are negative in the exact ratio, making the bound positive. This original sampling calculation illustrates one barrier to enumerative assurance: rare mechanisms are expensive to rule out through ordinary tests. It assumes independent cases and a known failure probability; adversarially selected situations need a different analysis.

The review's axes of progress clarify why complete enumeration may be unnecessary for some applications. A narrow monitor can be useful with partial understanding. Broad verification demands coverage of a much larger task distribution. Understanding a fixed checkpoint differs again from predicting how its mechanisms evolve during training.

Evaluate the goal, not just the microscope

The review surveys monitoring, control, behavioral prediction, training improvements, scientific knowledge extraction, broader model families, and human interaction with internals. These goals reward different properties. A scientific explanation may be valuable without winning a deployment benchmark; an engineering claim should be compared with competitive alternatives under matched budgets.

For example, a feature-based monitor should be evaluated at specified false-positive rates on shifted distributions and compared with input-output monitors. A model edit should be assessed for intended changes and collateral damage. A compelling circuit story is not a substitute for either comparison. Conversely, useful prediction alone may not establish a mechanistic explanation.

The socio-technical discussion asks how technical results connect to governance and warns about selective transparency and exaggerated communication. Its legal and policy examples are historical illustrations from the review, not current legal guidance. Localized successes cannot justify the claim that a model is fully understood or safe under all relevant conditions.

How to use a review as evidence

The paper maps unresolved questions and synthesizes competing priorities. Its cited demonstrations support particular examples; its prioritization judgments remain judgments. The source explicitly notes that the synthesis need not reflect every author's or institution's view. A useful reading separates proposed research directions from already tested methods and treats later papers as opportunities to update individual entries.

The review supplies a demanding standard for this collection: state the explanatory object, the task distribution, the type of validation, and the practical objective. “More interpretable” without those qualifiers can conceal trade-offs rather than describe progress.

Exercises

  1. Conceptual: Give one use for a probe that need not identify a causal mediator, and one claim that does require causal evidence.
  2. Mathematical: For $p=0.01$ and $\delta=0.05$, compute the minimum integer sample size under the independent-detection model.
  3. Critical experiment: Design an interpretability benchmark with both known mechanisms and a competitive non-interpretability baseline.
