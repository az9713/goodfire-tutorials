How straight dictionary atoms encode a curved concept

Can a sparse autoencoder represent a manifold? Yes: reconstruction by straight directions does not require the data themselves to lie on straight lines. The source distinguishes three ways an SAE distributes a curved concept across its latents and develops a pipeline that groups statistically related latents into candidate concept subspaces.

Preparation: linear combinations, statistical dependence, sparse coding and manifolds. Continue to block-sparse featurizers or capacity allocation.

Expressivity is not interpretive alignment

Let $x\in\mathbb R^d$ be a model activation. An SAE encodes it as sparse coordinates $z(x)\in\mathbb R^m$ and reconstructs $\hat x=b+Dz(x)$, where $D$ contains decoder directions and $b$ is a bias. The coefficients can vary nonlinearly with $x$. A curved family of inputs therefore can be reconstructed through a fixed linear decoder: the coefficient vectors themselves trace a curved family.

The interpretive hope that one latent equals one complete concept is a stronger assumption. A latent may respond only to cold conditions while others respond to warm or extreme heat conditions. Such labels can be locally informative while hiding the continuous temperature organization joining them. This is the source's central distinction: successful pointwise reconstruction need not expose the global object a researcher wants to understand.

Three capture regimes

The source trains SAEs on synthetic mixtures containing curved structures and changes reconstruction sparsity. In shattering, local points or small neighborhoods receive distinct latents, tiling a manifold. In compact capture, a small shared coordinate set represents the whole manifold. Dilution lies between these extremes: partially shared features cover overlapping regions. These are descriptions of latent support patterns, rather than three possible topologies of the original manifold.

A useful diagnostic records which latents are active on each example. For $N$ examples define $A_{nj}=1$ if latent $j$ is active on example $n$, and zero otherwise. Statistical association between columns can suggest a common underlying factor. However, raw coactivation favors frequent features, and mutually exclusive local tiles may have negative rather than positive association. The downloaded explainer says the pipeline clusters statistical dependencies; it does not justify replacing that specification with a particular universal correlation threshold.

Once a group $G$ is proposed, inspect activations in $\operatorname{span}\{D_j:j\in G\}$. This is an enclosing linear space. Recovering a manifold requires studying the occupied subset inside it, not treating every point in the span as naturally occurring. Clustering is a hypothesis generator, and causal controls are still needed.

Original worked example: one circle, two dictionaries

This exact construction is teaching material, not a reproduction of the synthetic training. Let $x(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$. With signed coordinates, two dictionary atoms $e_1,e_2$ reconstruct the entire circle. If codes must be nonnegative, use four atoms $e_1,-e_1,e_2,-e_2$ and coordinates

$$z=(\max(\cos\theta,0),\max(-\cos\theta,0),\max(\sin\theta,0),\max(-\sin\theta,0)).$$

Subtracting the opposing positive parts recovers each component exactly. At $\theta=\pi/4$, only the first and third coordinates are active, both $1/\sqrt2$. At $3\pi/4$, the second and third are active. The atoms do not each describe the whole circle, yet together they preserve it exactly.

Alternatively, place $K$ unit atoms uniformly around the circle and approximate a point by its nearest atom with coefficient one. Maximum angular discrepancy is $\pi/K$, and squared Euclidean error is $2-2\cos(\pi/K)\approx\pi^2/K^2$. Increasing $K$ improves this local tiling without adding a new semantic factor. This demonstrates why dictionary growth can improve reconstruction while mostly refining an already discovered concept. Actual SAE optimization may find different codes; the construction proves possibility, not inevitability.

Evidence and limitations

Known synthetic geometry permits a direct test of recovery. The source's Llama 3.1 8B examples are more exploratory: automatic grouping surfaces meaningful manifolds, but labels and dimensionality reduction still require validation. Shared document topic, token position, or lexical frequency can connect otherwise different factors. Conversely, a concept appearing in disjoint contexts can be split across clusters.

The source concludes that post-processing SAE directions is useful but may be inefficient, motivating architectures designed for manifold discovery. It does not claim every SAE feature is meaningless or that geometry alone explains computation. The operations that read and write the manifold remain a separate mechanistic question.

Exercises

  1. Conceptual: Explain why exact reconstruction by an SAE does not imply one latent per concept.
  2. Mathematical: For nearest-atom circle reconstruction with $K=6$, calculate the worst squared error exactly.
  3. Critical experiment: How would you test whether a feature cluster reflects temperature rather than shared weather vocabulary?
