Source 34 · Preliminary research update · “Finding the Tree of Life in Evo 2”

Evolutionary distance as a ruler for neural geometry

Can a genomic model's hidden geometry reflect relationships among species? The source averages Evo 2 activations across genomic regions, compares distances with an external phylogeny, and learns a lower-dimensional metric. The scientific issue is whether the geometry reflects useful evolutionary organization, and what sequence statistics explain it.

Preparation: cosine similarity and pseudoinverses, confounds, validation splits, geodesics.

What is being embedded?

A species-averaged embedding is not an aligned gene sequence. The source independently samples random genomic regions for each species, takes hidden activations after sufficient preceding context, and averages them. This targets species-level signals that survive averaging: nucleotide composition, codon preferences, repeated motifs, and related properties. It can wash out localized biological functions.

The external phylogenetic distance provides a reference derived from evolutionary relationships. A UMAP plot showing taxonomic clusters is exploratory evidence; the more consequential tests compare measured distances and hold out related groups. Species are not independent identically distributed points: shared ancestry makes random train-test splits particularly easy to overinterpret.

A neighborhood graph as a curved ruler

For nonzero embeddings $x_i,x_j$, angular distance is $a_{ij}=\arccos[(x_i^\top x_j)/(\|x_i\|_2\|x_j\|_2)]$. Connect nearby embeddings in a nearest-neighbor graph and weight each edge by $a_{ij}$. Graph distance $d_G(i,j)$ is the minimum sum of edge weights over paths joining the species. It approximates travel along the occupied set rather than taking a direct ambient shortcut.

The source chooses the smallest neighbor count yielding a connected graph and finds graph distances correlated with phylogenetic distances. Connectivity alone does not validate the approximation: too many edges can bridge distant branches, while too few can follow sampling noise. Uneven species density and unusual groups affect the inferred paths. The source specifically discusses an outlying bacterial order with atypical genomic properties.

Learning a flatter comparison space

The source also learns $z=W(x-b)$ and predicts evolutionary distance by a scaled angular distance between transformed embeddings. It reconstructs using $\hat x=W^+z+b$, where $W^+$ is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. A reconstruction term discourages a distance-only embedding from ignoring how much original variance it retains.

For a Euclidean version of metric learning, $\|W(x_i-x_j)\|_2^2=(x_i-x_j)^\top M(x_i-x_j)$ with $M=W^\top W\succeq0$. This derivation explains why a linear map defines a learned metric. The source actually uses normalized angular comparisons, so scale cancels inside each cosine; the Euclidean identity is a teaching analogy, not its exact fitted loss.

Because the transform is supervised by phylogenetic distances, success demonstrates recoverability and generalization under that supervision. It does not mean the raw model already uses this exact coordinate system in its next-nucleotide computation.

Original worked example: ancestry and split leakage

Imagine species A and B are close relatives and C and D form another close pair. Set tree distances $d(A,B)=d(C,D)=2$ and cross-pair distances equal to 10. A random split placing A and C in training and B and D in testing lets a learned metric exploit nearly represented clades. Holding out both C and D instead asks whether the metric transfers to a new branch.

Now suppose embeddings contain a clade coordinate plus a GC-content coordinate. If GC content tracks the tree in training, a distance learner can succeed without identifying a deeper biological mechanism. Evaluate species whose GC content and ancestry disagree to distinguish these explanations. This is an original study-design example, not a report about the source's particular species.

What the update supports

The source uses clade holdouts, reports a gap between fitted and held-out distance correlations, and examines genome statistics as predictors of the learned subspace. Its “genomic style” account treats composition as a substantive biological signal rather than dismissing it as meaningless noise. But composition can also confound claims about phylogenetic abstraction or functional knowledge.

A causal next step would intervene on candidate subspaces and measure nucleotide predictions or task-relevant outputs while preserving unrelated sequence properties. That would test use rather than accessibility. The research update is preliminary and does not establish a uniquely recovered tree, a universal genomic ontology, or clinical utility.

Exercises

  1. Conceptual: Why does species averaging emphasize different information from a gene-level embedding?
  2. Mathematical: Show that $M=W^\top W$ is positive semidefinite and explain what happens to differences in the nullspace of $W$.
  3. Critical experiment: Propose a split that tests ancestry-related structure beyond GC composition.
