Make a small model change easier to see

A rare failure introduced by fine-tuning may be invisible in a small evaluation sample. The August 21, 2025 article proposes model diff amplification: sample from logits that exaggerate the post-training change. A November 2025 update notes that the method is also called logit diff amplification (LDA). This is a discovery procedure; amplified samples do not estimate the deployed model's failure prevalence. Optional preparation: rare-event statistics and logits and sampling.

An extrapolation in log-probability space

For a shared context $c$, let $z_{0j}(c)$ and $z_{1j}(c)$ be pre- and post-training logits for token $j$. With amplification coefficient $\alpha\gt0$, define $z_{Aj}=(1+\alpha)z_{1j}-\alpha z_{0j}$. Both models must process the same evolving context after each sampled token. This is not a one-time interpolation of independently generated responses.

Let $p_0$ and $p_1$ be the two softmax distributions at unit temperature. Their log probabilities equal logits minus context-dependent normalizers. Those constants cancel in the amplified softmax, giving

$$q_j(c)=\frac{p_{1j}(c)^{1+\alpha}/p_{0j}(c)^\alpha}{\sum_k p_{1k}(c)^{1+\alpha}/p_{0k}(c)^\alpha}.$$

Thus tokens with increased likelihood ratios are favored. For any pair of tokens $j,k$, amplified odds are the post-training odds multiplied by the change in odds raised to $\alpha$. The method can amplify benign style changes, tokenizer-related artifacts, and incoherence as well as harmful behavior. Nothing in the formula specifically identifies harm.

At nonunit temperature, the exponent is additionally rescaled. Top-$p$ truncation changes support and further complicates importance-weight corrections. Because contexts depend on prior amplified choices, a sequence probability is a product of context-dependent conditionals, not a simple normalized power of two whole-sequence probabilities with one global constant.

Original worked example: rare token odds

Take an invented binary next-token event with pre-training probability $p_0=0.01$ and post-training probability $p_1=0.02$. Their odds are $1/99$ and $1/49$. At $\alpha=1$, amplified odds are $(1/49)^2/(1/99)=99/2401$, so amplified probability is $99/2500=0.0396$. The training change doubled the probability approximately; amplification nearly doubles it again. This is a one-step arithmetic illustration, not an estimate of a multi-token harmful trajectory.

If a natural failure probability were $10^{-4}$, independent sampling would require $n\ge\log(0.05)/\log(1-10^{-4})\approx29,956$ rollouts for a 95% chance of at least one failure. If a discovery distribution increased it to $1/30$, only 89 would suffice. This calculation explains the practical attraction of amplification. It does not allow one to use the amplified frequency as the natural rate.

The case studies and their boundaries

The source studies diluted emergent-misalignment training, early detection of a post-training regression, and sleeper-agent behavior. In its medical-advice mixture experiment, 5% problematic training advice produces about one harmful rollout in 10,000 under ordinary sampling and about one in 30 under amplification. Its early-training case uses OLMo 2 1B and a selected harmful prompt. These are preliminary experiments on particular settings, not a systematic sensitivity guarantee for all rare failures.

The backdoor case surfaces unwanted output without knowing the trigger, but the tested sleeper agents already leak the behavior occasionally without the trigger. The source explicitly identifies that limitation. A perfectly trigger-isolated backdoor might leave no useful off-trigger logit difference. Absence of amplified evidence would therefore not establish absence of a backdoor.

Coefficient selection is important. The appendix uses $\alpha=20$ for the small early-training change but notes that larger changes require weaker amplification to preserve coherence. Holding $\alpha$ fixed across training can confound increasing failure propensity with increasing distortion. Evaluate coherence separately and retest discovered prompts on the unamplified model.

Amplification changes the sampling distribution rather than the model's parameters. It is also distinct from parameter decomposition and activation probes. It can prioritize where to investigate, while other tools measure prevalence, trace causes, or mitigate failures. A good audit records model versions, shared tokenization, temperature, top-$p$, amplification strength, judge criteria, and unamplified confirmation.

Exercises

  1. Concept. Why must discovered failures be rechecked without amplification?
  2. Math. For the binary example, what happens if pre- and post-training probabilities are equal? What happens at $\alpha=0$?
  3. Experiment. Propose an evaluation that penalizes an amplifier for producing incoherent false alarms.
