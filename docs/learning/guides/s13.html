Measure a sentence's probability without waiting for it

Evaluation awareness is often measured by counting reasoning traces that explicitly mention being tested. But a model may assign elevated probability to such a sentence without sampling it. This post proposes scoring a prespecified evaluation-aware phrase at sentence boundaries. Its central claim is empirical: in the tested settings this likelihood-based readout distinguishes evaluation prompts and predicts later verbalizations with fewer sampled rollouts than a text judge.

Prerequisites: logits and softmax and classification metrics. For context $c$ and phrase tokens $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, a logit is an unnormalized next-token score. Softmax converts logits into conditional token probabilities. Teacher forcing means evaluating each intended phrase token while supplying the preceding intended tokens, rather than sampling a continuation.

Original derivation: phrase likelihood and sampling cost

The probability that the next $m$ tokens equal this exact token sequence is given by the probability chain rule:

$$P(u_{1:m}\mid c)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}P(u_j\mid c,u_{1:j-1}).$$

For stable computation, sum log probabilities: $\ell(c)=\sum_j\log P(u_j\mid c,u_{1:j-1})$. This is exact for the specified token sequence under the specified model distribution. It is not the probability of the entire semantic event “the model recognizes evaluation,” which includes other wordings, implicit representations, and possibly no verbalization.

As an original toy example, three conditional probabilities $0.2$, $0.5$, and $0.1$ yield phrase probability $0.01$. A second context giving $0.1$, $0.2$, and $0.1$ yields $0.002$, a likelihood ratio of five. Sampling can easily miss the first phrase: with 20 independent continuations, its absence probability is $0.99^{20}\approx0.818$. Direct likelihood scoring reads a graded signal even when no sampled trace contains the phrase.

However, a phrase score has its own choices. Changing phrase length changes its probability scale. Taking a maximum over more sentence boundaries increases opportunities for a high score. Comparing long evaluation traces with short deployment traces can therefore confound context type and the number of measurements. A start-of-reasoning score avoids this particular length confound, though it can still exploit prompt style.

A useful readout with a narrow interpretation

“Methods” describes token-forcing a fixed phrase and multiplying its conditional probabilities. “Results” compares this score with an LLM judge's fraction of evaluation-aware rollouts. The post reports an AUROC of 0.79 for one-rollout evaluation-versus-deployment discrimination, compared with a verbalization baseline approaching 0.75 at 100 rollouts in the main comparison. It also finds informative scores before any reasoning is sampled. Those are task-specific ranking results, not calibrated probabilities of hidden awareness.

Appendix B notes a direct confound: Petri prompts include system prompts whereas WildChat examples do not. A classifier can exploit that distinction without isolating awareness. The result that within-prompt verbalization explains little score variance supports a prompt-level interpretation, but does not prove that awareness cannot emerge during a trajectory. Variance decomposition depends on the chosen score, datasets, and phrase.

The score is complementary to activation probes. A phrase likelihood has an interpretable output event, while an activation classifier can potentially read information unrelated to verbalization propensity. Neither is automatically a causal account. The separate intervention study tests how inserting or removing explicit evaluation-aware text changes refusal; this post mainly measures prediction and discrimination.

A careful replication would freeze phrase selection before testing, match system-message structure and trace lengths, hold out benchmark families, and compare a phrase ensemble against single-phrase scores. Phrase selection after inspecting benchmark separability risks another form of evaluation overfitting. Calibration, threshold performance, and expert label audits should complement AUROC.

Reading route and capability

Read “Methods,” the three main “Results” claims, “Discussion,” and Appendices A–C. You should derive exact sequence probability, explain why that event differs from semantic awareness, and separate sample efficiency from compute efficiency. A forced phrase still requires model computation; zero sampled rollouts does not mean zero forward passes.

Exercises

  1. Why can an exact phrase probability be interpretable without being a calibrated awareness probability?
  2. A four-token phrase has conditional probabilities $0.4,0.2,0.5,0.1$. Find its probability and the chance of observing it at least once in 100 independent continuations.
  3. Design a control for prompt-format separability.
