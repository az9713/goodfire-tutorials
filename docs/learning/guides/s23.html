Two sequences can share a centroid and differ in function

A genomic model supplies one embedding per token, but a downstream task often needs one vector per gene. Mean pooling is stable and cheap, yet it loses whether represented properties occur together at the same positions. This methods essay proposes second-order pooling and a learned low-dimensional bottleneck. Its conceptual contribution is a precise information distinction: a token cloud's shape can contain useful evidence beyond its centroid.

Prerequisites: matrix algebra and singular values and embeddings and supervised probes. Let $X\in\mathbb R^{T\times d}$ contain $T$ token embeddings as rows. Mean pooling produces $\mu=T^{-1}\sum_t x_t$. The uncentered second moment is $C=T^{-1}X^\top X$. The centered covariance is $C-\mu\mu^\top$. The source's method generally uses the uncentered object, despite the convenient name “covariance pooling.”

Original worked case: co-occurrence survives pooling

Interpret two coordinates as detectors for a motif and a local structural context. Consider the invented activation matrices

$$X_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad X_B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Both have mean $(1/2,1/2)$. But their second moments differ:

$$C_A=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad C_B=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

The off-diagonal entry is the mean product of the two coordinates. It is $1/2$ for co-occurrence and zero for separation. A linear classifier on the mean cannot distinguish these examples; a linear classifier on the flattened second moment can. This is a representation-capacity statement for this pair, not proof that second-order pooling always improves generalization.

Second-order pooling still discards token order. For any permutation matrix $P$ acting on rows, $(PX)^\top(PX)=X^\top P^\top PX=X^\top X$. It therefore cannot distinguish different orders of the same embedding rows. Contextual embeddings may already encode position and surrounding sequence, but pooling itself adds no new order information. “Relational structure” here means relations between coordinates at represented tokens, not arbitrary long-range sequence relations.

Why a learned bottleneck is needed

A full $d\times d$ matrix can be enormous. Let $L,R\in\mathbb R^{d\times k}$ be learned projections with $k$ much smaller than $d$. The compressed matrix is

$$M=\frac1T(XL)^\top(XR)=L^\top CR\in\mathbb R^{k\times k}.$$

With $d=4096$ and $k=32$, the raw matrix has 16,777,216 entries and the compressed one 1,024. Projection and accumulation require approximately $O(Tdk+Tk^2)$ work, avoiding explicit construction of $C$ during supervised pooling. The projection parameters themselves still require $2dk$ entries. With untied $L,R$, $M$ need not be symmetric or positive semidefinite.

The essay also invokes $\|X^\top X\|_F=\|XX^\top\|_F$. To verify it, write the singular values as $s_i$. Both squared Frobenius norms equal $\sum_i s_i^4$. This identity concerns norms of two Gram matrices; it does not by itself prove equivalence of every reconstruction objective or eliminate all memory costs. The implementation's unsupervised target explicitly constructs a $d\times d$ matrix, so “linear time” is with respect to sequence length at fixed dimensions.

Useful results with an acknowledged scale limit

“Results: Covariance pooling outperforms mean pooling on downstream tasks” reports NTv3 experiments on genomic tracks and Gene Ontology labels. The stated 52.9% improvement in track-prediction $R^2$ and 8.4% AUC lift are relative reported improvements; they are not absolute increases of 0.529 and 0.084 unless the underlying baseline and convention establish that. The source calls these small-scale sanity checks.

More parameters and second-order interactions can also overfit small label sets. A fair comparison matches training data, split units, hyperparameter-search budget, and classifier capacity. Compare against alternative nonlinear probes on mean embeddings, as well as random or fixed low-rank projections. Biological labels, gene families, and genomic neighborhoods can leak across splits. Better annotation prediction remains an application result; it does not establish a causal regulatory mechanism.

Reading route and capability

Read “Mean pooling discards the combinatorial logic of genomic regulation,” “Learning embeddings with and without supervision,” “Implementation,” and the results section. You should distinguish means, uncentered moments, centered covariance, and untied projected cross-moments. The local labs make the information loss and projection arithmetic inspectable.
