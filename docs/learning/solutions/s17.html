Worked solutions · S17

Predicting Rare LLM Failures with 30× Fewer Rollouts — LessWrong

These answers are available from the start. Work the exercises before reading them if you want an independent self-check.

Worked answers

  1. Each token's normalizer depends on its context, and contexts change along generated trajectories. Failure is an aggregate event over many sequences. Summing their probabilities need not preserve a log-linear form.
  2. At $0.5$ the exponent is $-3$, giving $0.001$. At $1$ it is $-5$, giving $0.00001$. These are conditional model predictions, not direct observations.
  3. Fit only lower-$\alpha$ points, predict reserved higher-$\alpha$ points, then compare with large independent binomial samples. Repeat across prompt families and model pairs, reporting calibration and relative or log-scale errors with sampling uncertainty. Include deliberately curved alternatives and account for model-forward-pass cost. Good intermediate fit alone cannot validate the final endpoint.
