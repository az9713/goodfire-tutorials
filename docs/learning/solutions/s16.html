A Geometric Calculator Inside a Neural Network

These answers are available from the start. Work the exercises before reading them if you want an independent self-check.

Worked solutions

  1. The circle could encode an input, an answer correlated with another computation, or a visualization artifact. One needs input-to-output transformation evidence and interventions that change the proposed computational variable with predicted effects.
  2. The least common multiple is 10. Both integers have residues $(0,4,4)$ and therefore identical coordinates. Add a longer period or contextual range information to distinguish them.
  3. Use matched questions with the same numerical operands. Swap naturally observed module activations representing two sums while holding other positions fixed, and predict the new arithmetic answer and corresponding month. Include off-module patches, unchanged-sum controls, and fluency checks. Compare natural-range swaps with amplified steering rather than conflating them.
