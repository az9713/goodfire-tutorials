Worked solutions

  1. Polysemanticity describes several apparent response families. A dimension count describes an encoding's capacity and dependencies. Arbitrary packing proposes why unrelated families share mechanisms. The essay chiefly disputes arbitrary packing as the dominant explanation and argues that the first observation does not imply it.
  2. $\operatorname{SiLU}(-2)=-2/(1+e^2)$, so $a=6/(1+e^2)\approx0.715$. The positive product came from two negative factors. Grouping it with positive-positive examples can merge distinct computational regimes even though the scalar output has the same sign.
  3. Train several widths on controlled factors with known similarity and co-occurrence, save checkpoints, and register predictions before inspection. Measure whether shrinking capacity groups semantically or structurally related factors, or instead groups unrelated anticorrelated factors. Match performance and training budget, inspect sign regimes, and test the read/write pathways. Use held-out counterexamples and blinded explanation judgments. Synthetic results establish the tested regime, not a universal verdict about frontier models.
