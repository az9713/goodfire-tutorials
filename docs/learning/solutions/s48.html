Worked solutions

  1. It can reveal an unexpected behavior, suggest examples and confounders, and help inspect what a learned probe direction contains. After obtaining representative labels, train and validate the dedicated monitor on the full relevant activations.
  2. Error energy is $\delta^2$ and total energy is $100+\delta^2$, so relative error is $\delta^2/(100+\delta^2)$. It tends to zero as $\delta$ tends to zero, while the label remains perfectly readable from raw activations for every positive $\delta$ in exact arithmetic and completely absent from $Z$.
  3. Use a small labeled sample and a nonlinear encoder learned from abundant unlabeled data. The encoded features may expose a nonlinear decision rule or regularize estimation better than a raw linear classifier. Compare held-out risk and learning curves. The unrestricted raw predictor could emulate the encoder-plus-classifier, but a finite linear model cannot necessarily do so.
