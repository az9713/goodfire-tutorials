Geometry, sparse coding, and Fourier representations

Background for the Goodfire essays. Read any chapter when useful; no placement test or progress gate is required.

Solutions

  1. By rotational invariance take the points at angles $-\Delta/2$ and $\Delta/2$. Their midpoint is $(\cos(\Delta/2),0)$ and its norm is $\cos(\Delta/2)$ on the specified interval. At $\Delta=\pi$ the points are antipodal and the midpoint is zero. Then angle is not defined by the midpoint.

  2. $5+4=9\equiv2\pmod7$, so the vector is $(\cos(4\pi/7),\sin(4\pi/7))$. Sine and cosine are $2\pi$-periodic; this periodicity, together with the addition identities, produces the modular representation.

  3. Check geometry in the original activation space and under alternative projections or held-out data, and test whether controlled movements along the fitted representation cause predicted behavioral changes. Also examine nuisance variables and whether the decoder or interpreter introduces the structure. A circle in a two-dimensional visualization alone establishes neither the full representation nor the network's causal algorithm.
