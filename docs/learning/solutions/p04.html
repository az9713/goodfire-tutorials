Probability, probes, evaluation, and uncertainty

Solutions

  1. Precision becomes $0.9(0.001)/[0.9(0.001)+0.05(0.999)]$. The numerator is $0.0009$ and the denominator $0.05085$, giving approximately $0.01770$. There are many more negative than positive cases, so even a moderate false-positive rate dominates the alert population. This approximation is computed by the local lab.

  2. The exact bound is $1-0.05^{1/1000}$, approximately $0.002991$. The rough bound is $0.003$. Both rely on the stipulated sampling assumptions; correlated trials or a shifted deployment distribution are outside that statement.

  3. Separate patients and acquisition sites where feasible, fit preprocessing on training data only, and keep an external cohort for final testing. Test whether activations predict site labels and whether disease prediction persists within site or across new sites. High random-split disease accuracy that collapses on a new-site split, alongside strong site prediction, supports a confounding concern. It does not alone identify the physical source of the artifact.
