Worked solutions · S32

Mixing Mechanisms: How Language Models Retrieve Bound Entities In-Context

These answers are available from the start. Work the exercises before reading them if you want an independent self-check.

Worked solutions

  1. Without intervention, group position, query identity, and target pointer agree. Different internal routes are observationally equivalent on those examples. Counterfactual combinations make their predictions diverge.
  2. Exponentials are $(1,2,4)$, so probabilities are $(1/7,2/7,4/7)$. Adding constant $c$ multiplies numerator and denominator by $e^c$ and leaves probabilities unchanged.
  3. Patch from a counterfactual whose target is absent from the original context. A context-resolved pointer should not directly output the absent entity. Repeat after the suspected retrieval layer: a later answer representation may copy it successfully. Include controls against generic suppression of absent words and against a failed patch.
