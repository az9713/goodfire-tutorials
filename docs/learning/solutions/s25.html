Worked answers

  1. An external decoder may extract information the model does not yet use. Later computation can verify, correct, or translate that information. The claim of uselessness requires task-level intervention results, not merely prediction of eventual answers.
  2. $0.8(0.967)=0.7736$, or 77.36%. The loss is $80-77.36=2.64$ percentage points, distinct from the 3.3% relative loss.
  3. Randomly permute option order and labels across train and held-out examples, keeping the semantic correct answer fixed. Compare prediction of semantic answer identity with prediction of output letter. Hold out questions entirely. A large collapse under relabeling indicates reliance on formatting or label-position correlations, although remaining performance still needs subject and difficulty holdouts.
