Solutions

  1. $R=(2.2-1.9)/(2-1.9)=3$. The score has overshot the clean score by $0.2$, and division by the small $0.1$ baseline difference magnifies the normalized effect. Report all three scores. If clean and corrupted scores were equal, restoration would be undefined.

  2. Retaining both gives $1$; retaining only the first gives $1$; retaining only the second gives $1$; retaining neither gives $0$. The joint ablation establishes that the retained computation depends on the pair, although neither single ablation establishes individual necessity.

  3. The proposed direction may have no task specificity, or the chosen intervention magnitude may damage the network nonspecifically in many directions. Sweep magnitude, match directions by norm and relevant activation statistics, and measure both target and unrelated tasks on held-out examples. A direction-specific target effect at magnitudes that preserve unrelated performance would distinguish the first explanation from broad damage more effectively.
