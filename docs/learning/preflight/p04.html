When does a good detector tell us something real?

A probe is an auxiliary predictor trained on internal activations. It can reveal information available in a representation, help detect a behavior, or expose a confounder. A successful probe is not automatically an explanation of how the original network uses that information. The monitoring and biological-model essays require both statistical and causal reasoning.

Let $h\in\mathbb{R}^d$ denote activations and $Y\in\{0,1\}$ a defined target label. A linear probe has score $w^Th+b$. A logistic probe maps the score to $\sigma(w^Th+b)$, where $\sigma(t)=1/(1+e^{-t})$. A threshold turns that score into a binary decision. The meaning of the decision depends on how the labels were obtained.

Decodable information can be unused information

Construct $h=(Y,Z)$ where $Z=Y$ on the observed population. Let the model's output use only the first coordinate and a probe use only the second. The probe predicts $Y$ perfectly. Intervening on the second coordinate changes the probe but leaves the stipulated model output unchanged. Perfect prediction therefore does not establish causal use of the probed coordinate.

The example is a logical counterexample, not a claim that all useful probes are misleading. It explains why an activation monitor can have practical value without recovering the complete computational mechanism. New distributions can also break the correlation $Z=Y$. Held-out examples must test the generalization claim of interest, rather than merely shuffle near-duplicates from the same contexts.

Precision depends on prevalence

Write $\pi=P(Y=1)$ for the prevalence of concerning cases. Let $r=P(\widehat{Y}=1\mid Y=1)$ be true-positive rate, also called recall, and $f=P(\widehat{Y}=1\mid Y=0)$ be false-positive rate. Bayes' rule gives

$$P(Y=1\mid\widehat{Y}=1)=\frac{r\pi}{r\pi+f(1-\pi)}.$$

The event of a positive prediction is partitioned by the two possible true labels. Its probability is $r\pi+f(1-\pi)$. The joint probability of a true concern and positive prediction is $r\pi$. Dividing the joint probability by the positive-prediction probability gives precision.

A hypothetical deployment. In a population of $10{,}000$ cases with prevalence $1\%$, there are $100$ positive cases and $9{,}900$ negative cases. Recall $90\%$ yields $90$ true positives. False-positive rate $5\%$ yields $495$ false positives. Only $90/(90+495)=2/13$ of the alerts are true concerns. These are constructed counts, not a Goodfire deployment result.

Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, or AUROC, summarizes score ranking across thresholds. It can be interpreted, with half weight on ties, as the probability that a randomly selected positive receives a higher score than a randomly selected negative. It does not choose an operating threshold or specify deployment precision. Read production cyber monitoring with the actual rates, prevalence assumptions, and cost of mistakes in view.

Zero observed failures is not zero failure probability

Assume $n$ independent trials, each with the same unknown failure probability $p$. The probability of seeing zero failures is $(1-p)^n$. A one-sided exact upper confidence bound after zero failures, at confidence level $1-\alpha$, solves

$$ (1-p_{\mathrm{upper}})^n=\alpha,\qquad p_{\mathrm{upper}}=1-\alpha^{1/n}.$$

The interpretation is a repeated-sampling confidence procedure under the binomial assumptions. It is not a posterior probability statement about a fixed $p$. Using $\log(1-p)\approx-p$ for small $p$ gives $p_{\mathrm{upper}}\approx-\log(\alpha)/n$. At $\alpha=0.05$, the numerator is approximately $3$, producing the familiar rough bound $3/n$.

Tokens within one reasoning trace are not independent trials of a deployment incident. Patients from one study, prompts from one template, and multiple runs from one model can share dependencies. The sampling unit and split design must match the inference. Searching many probes or interventions and reporting only the best also requires a fresh confirmation set or an appropriate multiplicity analysis.

A linear fit and its limitations

For a centered activation matrix $H\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ and real target vector $y\in\mathbb{R}^n$, ridge regression minimizes $\|Hw-y\|_2^2+\lambda\|w\|_2^2$, with $\lambda\gt0$. Differentiating and setting the gradient to zero gives $(H^TH+\lambda I)w=H^Ty$. The positive penalty makes the matrix invertible. Numerically, solve this linear system rather than explicitly forming its inverse.

The model can still exploit nuisance variables or mislabeled data. Select regularization on validation data and report final performance on a distinct evaluation set. For a biological claim, also ask whether the split separates patients, laboratories, cohorts, and acquisition conditions in a way that tests the proposed use.

  1. Keep recall $0.9$ and false-positive rate $0.05$, but reduce prevalence to $0.001$. Derive precision and explain why it changes.
  2. Derive the exact upper bound for $n=1000$ independent trials and no failures at confidence $95\%$. Compare it with $3/n$ without treating either as a deployment guarantee.
  3. Design a split and a negative control for a probe trained to identify a biological disease from foundation-model embeddings. What observation would suggest site-specific rather than disease-specific signal?
