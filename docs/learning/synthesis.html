From interpretation claims to research judgment

Different claims require different evidence

The collection spans descriptive anatomy, causal interventions, training interventions, operational prediction, and scientific discovery. These activities can reinforce one another, but success at one level does not establish all the others. The first synthesis task is to name the conclusion at the correct level.

Native neurons, sparse dictionaries, and geometry

Already Clean asks us to reconsider why early examples looked polysemantic and how much semantic structure native neurons preserve. The practical SAE guide introduces a learned sparse dictionary as a way to find useful directions. A World Inside Neural Networks puts multidimensional representations and operations at the center. These are different objects: one original coordinate, a learned dictionary coordinate, and a geometric set with a computation acting on it.

The mathematical distinction is immediate on a circle. The points $u(\theta)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)^T$ form a one-dimensional manifold, meaning one continuously varying local parameter specifies a point. Their linear span is two-dimensional: $u(0)$ and $u(\pi/2)$ are independent. Neither native coordinate alone identifies the angle. A sparse dictionary can approximate portions of this curve with many directions, but that dictionary does not turn the original representation into a list of independent one-dimensional causes. See P05 for the derivation and the measured fixture.

At a linear interface, replacing $h$ with $Ah$ and the readout $W$ with $WA^{-1}$ preserves $Wh$ for any invertible $A$. This identity establishes a representational ambiguity at that interface. It does not make all coordinate systems equally privileged in a fixed trained architecture: componentwise nonlinearities generally fail to commute with rotations. Interpretability therefore needs both algebraic awareness and evidence about the particular network. The right experiment compares native and learned coordinates on predefined tasks, not just which visualization looks cleaner.

A comparative test. Choose the same activation site, held-out examples, intervention norm, and output metric. Compare native-coordinate edits, SAE-direction edits, a geometry-aware edit, and random directions matched for activation statistics. Measure desired effect and collateral change separately. A win for one representation on one task supports that task-specific conclusion. It does not settle every feature ontology.

Circuits and parameter decompositions answer different questions

Circuit tracing asks which internal computations connect a chosen input distinction to an output. Parameter decomposition asks whether the learned weight tensors admit useful components that can be studied or selected. SPD/APD and VPD must retain their own objectives and constraints; similar terminology does not make them identical algorithms.

An exact decomposition alone is weak evidence. For any matrix $W$, the components $100W$ and $-99W$ sum to $W$. On an input $x$, removing the first component changes the output by $-100Wx$, while removing the second changes it by $99Wx$. These changes are large relative to a nonzero original output, but vanish when $Wx=0$. Thus reconstruction fidelity does not prove modular semantics or stable component-level interventions. Conversely, a small residual matrix can be invisible on a narrow test distribution and important on another direction. If $E=\operatorname{diag}(0,0.01)$, then $E(1,0)^T=0$ while $E(0,1)^T=(0,0.01)^T$. The parameter fixture checks both examples.

A persuasive mechanism needs more than a small reconstruction error: useful component specialization, intervention predictions, robustness across inputs, and explicit accounting for any computation left in residuals. The sources differ in what they measure. Their individual companions preserve those distinctions instead of replacing them with a common success label.

A strong predictor can be a weak mechanistic explanation

Let a hidden state be $h=(c,s)^T$, where $c$ is causally used by the model and $s$ predicts a label because of the training distribution. A probe can decode $s$ perfectly even if the output is $y=c$ and never reads $s$. Intervening on $s$ while holding $c$ fixed leaves $y$ unchanged. This construction separates decodability from causal use without diminishing the practical value of a predictor.

Deployment also adds a denominator problem. A monitor with true-positive rate $r$ and false-positive rate $f$ has precision $pr/[pr+(1-p)f]$ at harmful-event prevalence $p$. For $p=0.01$, $r=0.9$, and $f=0.05$, precision is only $2/13\approx15.38\%$. At $p=0.001$ it is about $1.77\%$. A headline ranking metric cannot supply these operational numbers. The relevant sources must be read with their session definitions, thresholds, prevalence, labelers, and action policy intact.

Monitoring work is therefore a bridge to decision theory and systems engineering. The decision is whether to escalate, block, continue, or gather more evidence; the utility depends on error costs and timing. A scientifically useful extension is to optimize a policy under an explicit cost matrix, then test it on a separate deployment-like stream. Do not tune the threshold on the stream used to claim final performance.

Interpretability becomes more demanding when it changes training

Predictive Data Debugging, Probe-based Data Attribution, RLFR, and Intentional Design take internal measurements toward intervention on learning. The central challenge changes: a diagnostic score must predict the consequences of a training decision, not merely describe existing behavior.

A local derivative is a conditional prediction. For a differentiable loss $L(\theta)$ and small update $\delta$, $L(\theta+\delta)=L(\theta)+\nabla L(\theta)^T\delta+O(\|\delta\|^2)$ when suitable local smoothness holds. Large updates, changed activation regimes, optimizer state, or altered data mixtures can invalidate the useful approximation. The linear preference fixture computes an exact gradient and checks it with finite differences; it does not establish long-run behavior for a large language model.

The decisive study pre-registers candidate training decisions, predicts their effects from internal measurements, runs those decisions, and evaluates held-out behavior with controls for compute and data volume. Negative predictions matter: a method that cannot identify when it will fail has a weaker claim to general guidance. This is where the essays' research ambition becomes an experimental-design problem.

The scientific-discovery bridge

Biological foundation models learn from observations generated by evolution, measurement pipelines, and sampling decisions. A feature associated with a biological function can be valuable for hypothesis generation even before its causal role is known. Predictive improvement on a variant assay, however, does not automatically establish a treatment effect or the direction of a biological mechanism.

A useful connection is to sufficient statistics. Mean pooling retains a first moment and discards some joint variation. For the two-point sequences $A=\{(-1,-1),(1,1)\}$ and $B=\{(-1,1),(1,-1)\}$, both means are zero. Their uncentered second moments have off-diagonal entries $+1$ and $-1$, respectively. The pooling fixture proves that an additional statistic can separate examples a mean cannot. It says nothing about whether that statistic is sufficient for a particular biological outcome. That requires an empirical task and an appropriate validation split.

One productive research habit is to write the biological experiment before the model explanation. Specify which perturbation, assay, comparison group, and observation would distinguish your favored account from a credible alternative. Then ask whether the model's feature supports that experiment. This reverses the temptation to tell a biological story around a visually appealing feature.

Three complete offline investigations

These are fixed assignments with explicit deliverables and interpretation criteria. They use supplied generated data and trained small networks. They test transfer of the essays' methods, not replication of the essays' full-scale models. Run python docs\learning\labs\run_lab.py --lab all first and preserve the receipts.

  1. Vision: from a filter to a causal account. Use the stripe CNN in run_lab.py. Write the data generator and all tensor shapes in your report. Predict single-channel and joint-channel ablation effects before viewing results. The saved run has perfect test accuracy and no accuracy drop after any one channel is removed. Explain why this fails to establish that the channels are irrelevant. Extend the script to test pairs, all channels, and graded scaling. Add a shifted generator with diagonal lines or changed noise. Deliver the intervention table, kernels, baseline accuracy, and a claim limited to observed data. A correct report discusses redundancy and off-distribution interventions; it does not call a semantic label a mechanism.
  2. Language computation: a minimal routing transformer. Study the trained three-token network: two values and a selector specifying which value to output. Reconstruct embeddings, positional vectors, attention, residuals, feed-forward block, and readout from the code. Compare selector erasure with value-position erasure on the same held-out examples. Separate effects on selector-left and selector-right cases. Add matched clean/corrupt activation patches and random-position controls. Deliver a causal graph with predictions for untested swaps. Full credit requires correct shapes, split integrity, and acknowledgment that this finite generated task is not natural-language understanding.
  3. Unfamiliar-network transfer. Change either generator before training: encode selection with a new token order or classify stripe width rather than orientation. Freeze the evaluation criteria before looking at the learned activations. Compare a native coordinate, a linear probe, and a small sparse representation on the same site. Deliver one failed hypothesis, its discriminating observation, and a revised test. Success means the revision follows evidence; high accuracy alone is insufficient.

Solution framework for the capstones

The answers depend on the extension you run, so no invented results are supplied. The invariants are checkable: input generators and splits must be recorded; full-model accuracy comes before interventions; all interventions use the same examples; desired effects and collateral damage are reported separately; fitted probes never see test labels during training; and causal claims stay local to the tested intervention. For the saved vision run, single-channel ablation accuracy of 1.0 is consistent with redundant sufficient channels, small margin changes that do not alter labels, or compensation in the readout. It cannot distinguish those accounts. For the saved routing run, lower accuracy after erasure supports sensitivity to that erasure, while zeroed vectors can be off-distribution. Clean/corrupt patching with selector-conditioned analysis is a stronger next test.

A complete final oral explanation should connect S02's interpretation debate to S04's geometry, S05's parameter components, S03's prospective training predictions, and S06's design agenda. State one defensible agreement between them and one unresolved tension. Use a specific experiment to support each statement. That is the course's standard of mastery.
